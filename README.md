# The Crumbs Economy Made Trenches

A general description of the American economy under fictional American President Crumbs. Generated by Claude Sonnet 4.6.
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

President Crumbs was not merely a figurehead. He presided over a nation that had inherited real contradictions — the difficulty of balancing expansion against integration, multinational trade against cultural coherence, the promise of technological supremacy against a labor force that had been hollowed out for decades before he arrived. The administration had vocal critics, and those critics had merit. Certain problems were beyond what the Crumbs approach could resolve, and those problems did not stop existing because the administration lacked the tools or the inclination to address them.

---

During the first half of Crumbs' rule, the domestic social fabric had begun showing wear. His faction consolidated power by giving political cover and momentum to people who had every reason to be angry — disenfranchised loners, envious underdogs, the aggrieved and the crazed. Demonstrations followed. Walkouts. Entire areas of the nation were let go to waste through the managed withdrawal of institutional attention.

New families were hard to start. Careers were difficult to enter. People who would ordinarily keep their opinions to themselves found it harder to do so. When that group loses its composure, something structural has shifted.

The proposed solutions borrowed partly from Schinian practices — collectivist frameworks applied to a Western political tradition built on different foundations. The specifics of Schinian autocratic collectivism did not translate cleanly onto Western monarchism and statism. The result was policies that looked coherent on paper and produced confusion in practice.

---

During the second half of Crumbs' rule, the nation embarked on what was officially framed as an A.I. race against the Schinian mainland. The financial backers of Crumbs were nervous about their software advantages being undercut by Schinian industrialists who had proven capable of moving faster and cheaper than anticipated. What began as a strategic pivot became a mass panic. The panic dissolved many promising startup companies before they could become anything. The survivors were redirected into a nationalistic movement — "America First" in technological development, hardware and software both. Some of the workers within it were genuinely motivated by that framing. Others had darker and more self-interested reasons for participating, reasons that did not get mentioned in official communications because they were not useful for national unity.

Men such as Mario Amador, described in [A synopsis of the fictional American President Crumbs' administration](https://github.com/Changissnz/the_president_crumbs_era_in_one_nutshell__claude_generated), managed to stay on top through this period, though the disinformation and instability surrounding artificial intelligence during these years was not an easy environment to operate in, even for those doing relatively well within it.

---

There were serious concerns underneath the nationalistic momentum.

Many of the specialists used to build and supervise the foundational architecture of these A.I. systems were not American-born. When Crumbs' decentralization program produced waves of mass layoffs across the United States bureaucracy, some of those foreign experts returned to their countries of origin. They brought their knowledge with them, including working familiarity with the systems they had helped construct. The administration did not appear to be looking for a clean outcome in these arrangements.

The military was still having difficulty integrating A.I. into its practices. In actual conflict conditions, the advantages of superior A.I. systems could erode quickly. The systems depended on supply chains, on human operators, on a web of interdependencies that did not simplify under pressure. This was understood quietly by those familiar with the technical realities. It was not a popular point to raise in official settings.

The steady advancement of A.I. also posed a longer-term problem. As these systems improved at learning from and building on human knowledge, the expertise that had built them risked becoming less essential over time. There were differing opinions on how to handle this. The development continued regardless.

---

Media had been democratized by widespread internet adoption well before Crumbs took office. By the time he left, that democratization had produced a large volume of content and a significant amount of cultural loss alongside it. Creators and work of genuine quality from earlier periods became harder to find and harder to reach. The people who might have valued that work could not always get to it. This happened in part because democratization coincided with a broad decline in purchasing power. Ambitions that might have built something lasting were cut short before any real foundation formed.

---

The Crumbs era pushed what some were calling the trench model back into mainstream Western practice. The trenches were economic and regulatory — people found themselves unable to leave their county or state due to the specifics of finance and local regulation. Multinational trade agreements had begun breaking down. Opportunity narrowed. Risk kept rising.

The decline of Brittania, elaborated on in [An elaboration on the fictionalized Brittania during the second quarter of the 21st century](https://github.com/Changissnz/brittania_wanted_to_matter__claude_generated), shaped how the United States positioned its A.I. capabilities. Without Brittania functioning as a reliable partner, the United States leaned toward hard defense rather than offensive use of these technologies. A sustained offensive posture would have required stable supply chains and motivated human expertise. Neither could be counted on.

The democratization that had helped build A.I. technology also meant the United States held no exclusive claim to it. Against a serious national adversary, the country could expect to be one or two steps ahead at most. Against an adversary unwilling to engage on conventional terms, even that margin was uncertain.

The country had entered a period shaped partly by A.I. and partly governed by it. The conditions that got it there — debt, regulatory immobility, fragile supply chains, departed experts, dissolved companies, declining institutions — had been building for years before Crumbs arrived. His administration did not create all of them. It did, however, make the hole considerably deeper.
